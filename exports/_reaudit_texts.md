### content/grammar/chapter-00/chapter.json.pattern.body [teaching]

### content/grammar/chapter-00/chapter.json.nerd.body [teaching]

You do not speak it because languages keep changing. Parents slur, kids copy the slur, and after enough centuries the extra endings fall off. A later mix with French in England sped that wearing-down up, so the English in your mouth is the later, simpler version that won.

That older English hung extra letters on the end of a thing-word to show the job, the same habit Bosnian still has. You can still see a scrap of it when you say I if you do the thing and me if the thing happens to you. Coffee lost those outfits, so this is coffee and I want coffee stay the same. Bosnian never fired the endings on kahva. English moved the work off the word. Bosnian left it on.

### content/grammar/chapter-01/chapter.json.pattern.body [teaching]
You guess the pile from the last letter of the list word, which is the boring form sitting in a vocab list. A word that ends in a usually belongs to the she pile, such as kahva, kuća, and ulica, because those words joined an old a club. A word that ends in a consonant usually belongs to the he pile, such as most, dan, and hljeb. A word that ends in o or e usually belongs to the leftover pile, such as pivo, more, and Sarajevo. People skip that cheat sheet because Emir is a man and Ana is a woman. Keep the sheet for coffee, and remember the pile is a filing club, not a vibe.

### content/grammar/chapter-01/chapter.json.trick.body [teaching]

### content/grammar/chapter-01/chapter.json.nerd.body [teaching]

### content/grammar/chapter-12/chapter.json.englishLies.body [teaching]

### content/grammar/chapter-12/chapter.json.pattern.body [teaching]
Put one stressed word or short phrase first, then park the little word. Ja se zovem Ana puts Ja first and se second. Zovem se Ana puts Zovem first and se second. ### content/grammar/chapter-12/chapter.json.howYouGuess.body [teaching]

### content/grammar/chapter-12/chapter.json.nerd.body [teaching]
