## Culture — Baščaršija — Sarajevo’s old heart

Baščaršija is the historic bazaar of Sarajevo: narrow streets, copper shops, cafés, and the sound of footsteps on stone. Ana arrives with a backpack and a phrase list. Shopkeepers greet visitors with a warm Dobar dan! Learning greetings here is not just polite — it opens doors (and often a free smile). The special letters in Baščaršija (š, č) are your first real Bosnian workout.

## Grammar — The Latin Bosnian alphabet

Bosnian is written with the Latin alphabet in this book. Most letters sound familiar. Six special letters matter a lot: č, ć, š, ž, đ, and the digraph dž. We never use Cyrillic here.

- **čaj** — tea (č like English 'ch' in church)
- **ćup** — jar (ć is softer, closer to 't' + soft 'ch')
- **šuma** — forest (š like 'sh')
- **žena** — woman (ž like 's' in measure)
- **đak** — pupil (đ like 'j' in juice, softer)
- **džezva** — coffee pot (dž like 'j' in jam)

## Grammar — Greeting exchanges

Start simple. Say a greeting, hear one back, then add hvala or molim. Formal day greetings use dobar dan; friends often say zdravo.

- **Zdravo!** — Hello!
- **Dobar dan!** — Good day!
- **Hvala. — Molim.** — Thank you. — You're welcome.

## Lesson A — Letters that look new

Look at these six: č ć š ž đ dž. Pair each with a word you can feel in your mouth. Čaj (tea). Ćup (jar). Šuma (forest). Žena (woman). Đak (pupil). Džezva (the little pot for Bosnian coffee). Say them slowly. Ana practices on the plane: čaj, čaj, čaj — then walks into Baščaršija and sees a real džezva in a shop window.

## Lesson B — Your first greetings

Memorize this mini set: Zdravo (hi), Dobar dan (good day), Dobro jutro (good morning), Dobra večer (good evening), Hvala (thanks), Molim (please / you're welcome), Doviđenja (goodbye), Da / Ne (yes / no). Use Dobar dan with people you do not know well. Use Zdravo with friends — or with Emir, once you meet him tomorrow.

## Conversation — Ana’s first hello

_A narrow street in Baščaršija. Ana nearly trips over a cat — Mrvica, though she does not know the name yet._

**Ana:** Dobar dan!  
*Good day!*

**Shopkeeper:** Dobar dan! Molim?  
*Good day! Yes? / Can I help you?*

**Ana:** Hvala… čaj?  
*Thank you… tea?*

**Shopkeeper:** Da. Čaj. Molim!  
*Yes. Tea. Here you are! / Please!*

*Thank you! Goodbye!*

**Shopkeeper:** Doviđenja!  
*Goodbye!*

**Narrator:** Mrvica mjauče. Ana kaže: Zdravo!  
*Mrvica meows. Ana says: Hello!*

### Fun fact — Baščaršija means ‘main market’

The name comes from Turkish roots meaning the chief marketplace. Today it is still full of craft shops and cafés.

### Fun fact — One letter, one sound

Bosnian spelling is mostly phonetic: what you see is what you say. That helps beginners a lot once the special letters click.

### Fun fact — Cats of the čaršija

Sarajevo’s old town is famous for friendly street cats. Mrvica is fictional — but her attitude is very local.
